# 02 — Problem and Solution

## The Problem

Designing a city is a systems problem. A decision in one area affects many others.

For example:

- Higher density can improve public-transport efficiency but increase infrastructure pressure.
- Wider roads can increase vehicle capacity but reduce walkability.
- More green space can improve climate resilience but reduce developable land.
- Centralized services can reduce cost but increase travel distance.
- Rapid development can meet housing demand but create long-term maintenance risks.

Early-stage project ideas often arrive as broad ambitions rather than complete technical briefs. Turning them into a coherent urban strategy requires time, expert coordination, data, and repeated revision.

## Current Challenges

- Requirements are incomplete or contradictory.
- Planning disciplines work in separate tools.
- Trade-offs are difficult to compare.
- Decision reasoning may not be documented.
- Changes can break earlier assumptions.
- Non-specialists may struggle to understand technical plans.
- Scenario testing can occur too late.
- Previous versions and feedback may be lost.

## The Proposed Solution

AI Future Lab acts as an intelligent coordination layer.

It converts an unstructured idea into:

- Goals
- Constraints
- Priorities
- Assumptions
- Planning rules
- Spatial requirements
- Performance targets
- Validation criteria

The platform then coordinates planning, digital-twin construction, scenario analysis, explainability, and revision.

## Value Proposition

### For planners

A structured starting point, faster comparison, stronger traceability, and better communication.

### For developers

Earlier understanding of capacity, service needs, mobility, sustainability, and development trade-offs.

### For governments

A decision-support environment for exploring policy and planning scenarios.

### For students and researchers

A learning and experimentation platform for understanding cities as connected systems.

### For the public

Clearer explanations of how and why urban decisions are made.
